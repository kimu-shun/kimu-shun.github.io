## Mai Kawato (Leader)

### Project Proposal

#### One-Page Project Proposal

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_2.jpg)

It’s fun because you can’t move exactly how you want. We adopted a unique control system that feels intuitive while making it difficult to move exactly as intended. While tilting the Joy-Con to control the characters, players must coordinate with each other to create an experience that is “fun precisely because things don’t go as planned.”

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_3.jpg)

Our goal was not only to make the controls difficult to master, but also to create a control system that becomes more enjoyable as players get used to it.

### Specifications

#### Character Movement

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_4.jpg)

We made the Joy-Con’s tilt “visible.”
The ring around the character’s waist also moves in response to the tilt, making the current control state visually easy to understand.

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_5.jpg)

We created the effect in a way that would not detract from the character’s appearance.

#### Flowchart

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_6.jpg)

I created the flowchart for the entire game.
I focused on documenting in detail which screens the player can transition to from each screen.
I was also responsible for the game’s other general specifications documents.

#### Stage Design Documents

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_7.jpg)

This is the design document for Stage 1.
To help players get used to the game’s unique movement controls, this stage contains many areas where players jump from one platform to another.

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_8.jpg)

This is the design document for Stage 2.
To provide a challenge even for players who have become somewhat accustomed to the controls, this stage contains many areas where players must jump between moving platforms.

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_9.jpg)

This is the design document for Stage 3.
In this stage, players clear the level by moving and operating the gimmicks themselves.
It is also the stage with the strongest emphasis on cooperation between two players.

![image](data/img/plan/plannerImages_10.jpg)

These are the design documents for the tutorial and stage selection screen.
For the tutorial, I carefully documented the timing at which instructional text appears.
For the stage selection screen, I arranged the layout and paths so that players can naturally find the stages in the intended order of progression.
